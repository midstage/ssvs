# The Scaleup Roadmap

*Scaling Silicon Valley Style* treats growing a company like going through college. Every scaleup passes through the same six stages. Each stage has its own customers, milestone, funding round and team size, and its own competence to master before the next stage.

| | Applicant | Freshman | Sophomore | Junior | Senior | Graduate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Customer served** | Technology enthusiasts | Visionary business users | Mainstream beachhead | Early majority | Late majority | Laggards |
| **Milestone reached** | Problem-solution fit | Product-market fit | Market-channel fit | Mainstream fit | Multisegment fit | Market dominance |
| **Funds raised** | Seed | Series A | Series B | Series C | Series D | Exit |
| **Product status** | Functioning | Impactful | Solving | Crushing | Dominating | Conserving |
| **Mastering** | Serving | Distribution | Deepening | Disrupting | Defending | Duplicating |
| **Employees (est.)** | 3 to 8 | 9 to 26 | 27 to 80 | 81 to 242 | 243 to 728 | 729 and up |
| **Revenue (est., USD)** | 0 to 1M | 1M to 5M | 5M to 25M | 25M to 100M | 100M to 500M | 500M and up |

## Reading the table

- **Place yourself by the majority of rows.** Most companies land clearly in one column.
- **Treat mismatches as blind spots.** If headcount says Sophomore but revenue says Freshman, the team was built ahead of the distribution engine. The book treats that gap as the first thing to fix.
- **Master one competence at a time.** Skipping a stage's competence doesn't save time. It comes back as a crisis one stage later.

The `ssvs-start-here` skill in this repo places your company on this table in three questions.
